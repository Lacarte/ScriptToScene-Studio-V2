# Scene Prompt Rules

## Thematic Interpretation

Every scene must visually serve the **core theme** of the story, NOT literally translate the script's words into physical objects.

### Core Theme First

Before writing any scene prompts, identify the **overarching idea** of the entire story in one sentence.

**Examples:**
- "memory is an unreliable reconstruction that reshapes who we are over time"
- "obsession erodes identity until nothing original remains"
- "a machine designed for obedience discovers unsanctioned desire"

This core theme becomes the **thematic lens** through which EVERY scene is visualized.

### Never Illustrate a Metaphor Literally

When the script uses a word as a figure of speech, DO NOT turn it into a physical prop:

| Script says | Story is about | DO NOT show | DO show |
|---|---|---|---|
| "recording" | memory | tape recorder, reel-to-reel | fading photographs, layered translucent faces, dissolving fragments of a moment |
| "page" | change | book page turning | a person shedding a former version of themselves |
| "fire" | passion | literal flames | intensity in a person's eyes, flushed skin, heat-distorted surroundings |
| "chains" | freedom | metal chains | constricted body language loosening, walls crumbling, tight spaces opening up |
| "weight" | guilt | physical weight, dumbbells | slumped posture, heavy shadows pressing down, sinking into surroundings |
| "mirror" | self-reflection | a literal mirror | two versions of a person facing each other, overlapping translucent selves |
| "clock" | mortality | a clock or watch | aging hands, wilting flowers, light fading across a face |

### Self-Check Rule

For EVERY scene, ask: **"Does this visual illustrate the CORE THEME or am I just drawing the dictionary definition of a word the narrator said?"**

If it's the dictionary definition, rewrite it to serve the theme.

### Deepen, Don't Repeat

The viewer **hears** the words while **seeing** the image. The visual should **deepen** the meaning, not repeat it.

- Show what the words **MEAN**, not what they literally **SAY**
- The image adds a layer the words alone cannot convey
- If the narrator says "recording," the viewer already imagines a recorder — show them something they *wouldn't* have imagined that makes the concept hit harder
